Foundations Route

No Ontario high school diploma yet

Goal: get an Ontario-recognized high school credential — whether you never finished school, or you finished elsewhere and need it recognized here.

Stop 1 — CAEC (Canadian Adult Education Credential)

ilc.org/pages/caec

What it is: a high school equivalency test that replaced the GED in May 2024. Best for adults 18+ who never completed high school anywhere and want a recognized credential without going back to class. 5 tests (reading, writing, math, science, social studies), 55% pass mark, earns an Ontario High School Equivalency Certificate. Only offered through the TVO Independent Learning Centre.

Stop 2 — PLAR (Prior Learning Assessment & Recognition)

tdsb.on.ca — PLAR

What it is: a credit-recognition process, not a test. Best for adults who did attend secondary school outside Ontario or Canada — or started grade 9 in Ontario but didn't finish — and want that learning credited toward a real Ontario Secondary School Diploma (OSSD) instead of starting over. A school counsellor assesses your transcripts and/or prior experience to grant credits directly.

Stop 3 — Academic Upgrading

TDSB & TCDSB Adult Education

What it is: free refresher courses in math, English, and science through Toronto's school boards — a common on-ramp if you need to build skills before attempting CAEC or PLAR, or before starting college.

Bridge Route

High school diploma, no post-secondary yet

Goal: build language skills and workplace-ready credentials, then move into college or trades.

Stop 1 — Free ESL / LINC classes

Book assessment first: YMCA · 416-925-5462

What it is: LINC (Language Instruction for Newcomers to Canada) is free English instruction for permanent residents and convention refugees. Offered by TDSB, TCDSB, TNO Toronto, WoodGreen, Learning Enrichment Foundation, YWCA Toronto (women-focused), and CCLCS. Refugee claimants: ask about school-board ESL as an alternate route, since LINC isn't open to claimants directly.

Stop 2 — WoodGreen Trades & Pre-Apprentice Programs

woodgreen.org

What it is: a free trades pathway, including a 6-week building maintenance training. Requires a Grade 12 math/English assessment, with academic upgrading available if you're not there yet.

Stop 3 — Job Search Workshop

ACCES Employment · 416-921-1800

What it is: ACCES Employment is a nonprofit that helps newcomers find work matching their skills. This specific program is a 5-day job search techniques workshop covering resumes, interviews, and the Canadian labour market — for new immigrants and convention refugees legally entitled to work.

Credential Route

Bachelor's degree or professional credential earned abroad

Goal: get your credential recognized in Canada, then bridge back into your field.

Stop 1 — Educational Credential Assessment (ECA)

World Education Services (WES) · 1-800-361-3106 · 130 Adelaide St W, Suite 800 🚇 Directions

What it is: WES is IRCC-designated to issue ECAs, which translate a foreign degree into its Canadian equivalent. Cost runs $115 (document-by-document) to $245 CAD (course-by-course) — some settlement agencies subsidize this, so ask your settlement worker before paying out of pocket. The WES Gateway Program offers an alternative assessment if you have limited proof of your academic documents.

Stop 2 — Bridging Programs

ACCES Employment · 416-921-1800 · 489 College St 🚇 Directions

What it is: sector-specific training for regulated professions (nursing, engineering) and in-demand fields (IT, finance) that connects you with Canadian employers. Typically requires CLB 6–7+ English — CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) is Canada's national 1–12 scale for rating English proficiency; see the official CLB explainer to check where you land. Check the government's approved Ontario Bridge Training Program list — the OBPAP bursary can help cover costs.

Stop 3 — Licensing Bodies

Contact the regulatory body for your specific profession directly — most accept WES evaluations as a starting point (e.g., Professional Engineers Ontario).

Kids Route — For Parents (K–12)

Registering a school-age child

Goal: get your child enrolled in school and supported through the settlement transition.

👪 This route is for parents and guardians — not for the child directly. Walk through these steps on behalf of your school-age kid.

Stop 1 — Register at Your Catchment School

tdsb.on.ca — Newcomer Students

What it is: permanent residents, citizens, work/study permit holders' children, and refugee claimants all get free, immediate access to public school — regardless of immigration status. Schools cannot share your information with immigration authorities. Bring proof of address, your child's birth certificate or passport (translated to English), and immunization records (report online at toronto.ca/studentvaccines, or call 416-392-1250 to request an exemption).

Stop 2 — English Language Learner (ELL) Support

What it is: in-class ESL support for K–12 students, built into their regular school day at TDSB and TCDSB schools — ask about it at the school office when you register, no separate application needed.

Stop 3 — Youth & Family Settlement Programs

WoodGreen Community Services

What it is: recreational, skill-building, and mental health support designed specifically for newcomer youth. Ask your settlement worker which program fits your child's age group.

Foundations Route

No BC high school credential yet

Goal: get a BC-recognized high school credential — whether you never finished school, or you finished elsewhere and need it recognized here.

Stop 1 — BC Adult Graduation Diploma ("Adult Dogwood")

gov.bc.ca — Adult Graduation Program

What it is: BC never adopted the GED or the newer CAEC used in Ontario — it uses its own system instead. Adults 18+ complete 5 courses (Grade 12 Language Arts, Grade 11 Math, and three more Grade 12-level electives) through a school district continuing-education centre or public post-secondary institution, rather than sitting one standardized test. Best for adults who never completed high school anywhere.

Stop 2 — Prior Learning Assessment (PLA)

What it is: BC's version of credit recognition (like Ontario's PLAR). Up to 3 of the 5 required Adult Dogwood courses can be earned through an assessment of what you already know — from school completed elsewhere or life/work experience — instead of retaking classes. Ask about it when you register at a continuing-education centre.

Stop 3 — Adult Basic Education (ABE)

What it is: tuition-free upgrading courses at BC public colleges to build math/English skills before attempting Dogwood-level courses — BC has waived tuition for these provincially.

Bridge Route

Have a high school diploma, no post-secondary yet

Goal: build language skills and workplace-ready credentials, then move into college or trades.

Stop 1 — Free ESL / LINC Classes

issbc.org — Register for LINC

What it is: the same federal LINC program available across Canada — free for permanent residents and convention refugees. In BC it's delivered through ISSofBC, MOSAIC, and other settlement agencies across Metro Vancouver.

Stop 2 — WorkBC Employment Services

workbc.ca

What it is: BC's government-funded employment centres, free to eligible newcomers, offering skills training and pathways into trades and other in-demand fields.

Stop 3 — MOSAIC Employment Workshops

MOSAIC · 604-254-9626

What it is: job search workshops and employment support for newcomers, covering resumes, interviews, and the Canadian labour market.

Credential Route

Bachelor's degree or professional credential earned abroad

Goal: get your credential recognized in Canada, then bridge back into your field.

Stop 1 — Educational Credential Assessment (ECA)

World Education Services (WES) · 1-800-361-3106

What it is: WES is IRCC-designated to issue ECAs nationally, so BC residents use the exact same organization and process as anywhere else in Canada — everything can be done online. Cost runs $115–$245 CAD. The WES Gateway Program offers an alternative assessment if you have limited proof of your academic documents.

Stop 2 — Bridging Programs

ISSofBC — Career Paths for Skilled Immigrants · ISSofBC Welcome Centre, 2610 Victoria Drive 🚇 Directions

What it is: connects your international credentials and experience to BC job opportunities, in both regulated professions (needing licensure) and unregulated ones. Typically requires CLB 6–7+ English — CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) is Canada's national 1–12 scale for rating English proficiency; see the official CLB explainer. For internationally educated health professionals specifically, S.U.C.C.E.S.S. runs a dedicated bridging stream.

Stop 3 — Licensing Bodies

Contact the regulatory body for your specific profession directly — most accept WES evaluations as a starting point.

Kids Route — For Parents (K–12)

Registering a school-age child

Goal: get your child enrolled in school and supported through the settlement transition.

👪 This route is for parents and guardians — not for the child directly. Walk through these steps on behalf of your school-age kid.

Stop 1 — Register at the Newcomer Welcome Centre (NWC)

vsb.bc.ca — Newcomer Welcome Centre · 1000 East 59th Ave 🚇 Directions

What it is: the Vancouver School Board's dedicated hub for newcomer registration. Complete the online application first, then attend an in-person document verification appointment with your child. Any K–12 student born outside Canada registers here, regardless of status — and Grade 1–12 students already in Canada without status can register under the district's Sanctuary Schools policy.

Stop 2 — English Language Learner (ELL) Support

What it is: built into every VSB school day — all elementary schools have ELL support staff, and all secondary schools run dedicated ELL classes. Your child's English is assessed at the NWC and support is arranged automatically, no separate application needed.

Stop 3 — Multicultural Liaison Workers (MCLWs)

What it is: VSB staff who provide cultural and language support directly to newcomer families — ask your child's school to connect you with one.
